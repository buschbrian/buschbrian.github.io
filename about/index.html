About

Brian Busch, GISP

I'm a GISP-certified Senior GIS Analyst at the City of Millcreek, where the work splits fairly evenly between cartography and the systems and data underneath it. I administer an enterprise ArcGIS Online organization (1,600+ items, 50+ users), write Python pipelines that move parcel and permitting data into production SQL systems, and build custom web apps on the ArcGIS Experience Builder SDK — alongside public-facing maps like Millcreek's Future Land Use Map, a 2025 Esri UC Map Gallery finalist, and the city's Sidewalk & Trails Master Plan, a 2026 Esri UC Map Gallery finalist (write-up).

How I got here

I started at Millcreek in 2019 as its first dedicated GIS hire, building the city's first public ArcGIS Hub and a Python workflow that pushed roughly 22,000 parcels into production SQL systems for Building Services and Finance. From there the role kept absorbing more of the city's spatial infrastructure: a GNSS field program on Trimble RTK hardware, an FAA Part 107 drone program, a citywide stormwater inventory built for MS4 compliance, and the 2021–2023 migration off ArcMap. By 2024 that had become a Senior GIS Analyst role built around enterprise governance — metadata standards, org-wide sharing policy, and Millcreek's first Esri Enterprise Agreement — on top of the cartography and app work that was always part of the job.

I passed the GISP exam on the first attempt in December 2024 — a broader test than I expected, touching AEC/BIM and remote sensing/sUAS alongside the civil engineering and surveying side I work in day to day.

What I actually do

  • Enterprise ArcGIS Online administration — governance, metadata (ISO 19115-3), sharing policy, licensing
  • Python/SQL data pipelines moving parcel, permitting, and regulatory data into production systems
  • Custom web apps on the ArcGIS Experience Builder SDK, including a Node.js build integrating the Nearmap API
  • Field data collection — GNSS/RTK surveying and an FAA Part 107 drone program (DJI Phantom 4 RTK, Pix4D orthomosaic processing) supporting 4–5 engineering projects a year
  • Cartography and print-map layouts for public-facing planning documents

Outside of work

I keep a personal cartography and data-engineering practice going alongside the day job. In November 2024 I built a run of selected maps for the annual 30 Day Map Challenge, working almost entirely with Utah data and John Nelson's published Esri styles. More recently, I've been comparing the ArcGIS API for Python against GeoPandas/pandas on real Utah datasets, shipping a live reservoir-drought dashboard outside the Esri stack entirely — the clearest signal of where I'm trying to take this next: more data engineering, more enterprise data management, still grounded in the cartography that got me here.

Away from a screen, that same pull toward out-of-the-way places shows up as backroad exploring, hiking, winter trail running, and landscape photography — most of it in Utah's more remote corners.
