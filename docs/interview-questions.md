# Interview questions

This file collects the questions that unblock the content backlog. Each
question asks for one fact. Answer in plain sentences. Do not write
finished copy. A rough answer is enough, because the copy comes later.

The site sells consulting work. The reader is a city director or an agency
manager. That reader asks four questions about every project:

1. Did this person understand my problem?
2. Can I follow the method?
3. What changed because of the work?
4. What can this method not do?

Every question below serves one of those four.

## 1. Ask these about every project

Answer this set once for each project note.

- What did the organization do before this work? Describe the old process.
- Who asked for the work, and what did they ask for?
- What was the deadline, and what forced it? A law, a grant, or a meeting?
- What did you personally do? Name the parts other people did.
- Which software did you use, and which version?
- Which data did you use? Name the source and the date of the data.
- What did you deliver? A map, an application, a dataset, or a report?
- Who uses the result now, and how often?
- What is the result? Give a number if a number exists.
- What can the analysis not show? Name one real limit.
- What would you do differently now?
- Can a reader see the result in public? Give the link if one exists.

- How many other Utah cities still need this work?

## 7. The school bus service area analysis

This project is not on the site yet. Answer section 7.1 before any other
part of this section. If the answer to 7.1 is no, stop.

### 7.1 Clearance

- Who owns this work? You, an employer, or a client?
- Did you do the analysis as part of a job? Name the employer.
- Does a contract or an employee agreement limit what you can publish?
- Did the school board decide this in a public meeting? Give the date.
- Is the decision, the staff report, or the map already public record?
- Was there a complaint, an appeal, or a lawsuit? Is any part sealed?
- Who should you ask for permission? Name the person and the office.
- Will you name the district and the school, or keep them out?

### 7.2 The rule

- Which exact rule governs the walking distance? Give the full citation.
- Quote the sentence that sets the distance limit.
- Does the rule say what the measurement starts from and ends at?
- Does the rule require a walking route, or a straight line?
- Who interprets the rule when the text is not clear?
- Was the original analysis wrong about the rule, or only the measurement?

### 7.3 The measurement

- What did the original analysis measure to? The building corner?
- Why did it use that point? A tool default, or a choice?
- What did you measure to instead? Describe the access point.
- How far apart were the two points? You said about 75 feet south.
- Did you measure along the street network or in a straight line?
- Which network data did you use, and who maintains it?
- What were the two distances? You said 2.01 and 1.99641 miles.
- Which figure did the school publish, and which did you produce?
- How did you verify the access point? A site visit, or imagery?
- Does a sidewalk exist on the corrected route the whole way?
- Is the corrected route safe? Crossings, lighting, and traffic.

### 7.4 The finding and the decision

- How did you find the error? Who asked you to look?
- What did you produce to show it? Maps, a memo, or a presentation?
- Who did you present it to, and when?
- How did the district respond at first?
- Who made the final decision, and on what date?
- What changed? You said one more bus and one more driver.
- What did the change cost the district each year?
- How many students got transportation because of the correction?
- How long was the walk before? You said about 50 minutes each way.
- Did the district change its method for other schools too?

### 7.5 How to describe the community

The residents are a small group and a reader could identify them. Decide
the words before any draft.

- How do you describe the residents? Use their own words if you know them.
- Do you name the building, or only the general area?
- Does the note need the income and refugee detail to make its point?
- Who can review the wording before it goes public? A community leader?

### 7.6 What can be published

- Which maps can you publish as they are?
- Which maps hold internal data and must be rebuilt from public data?
- Can you publish the distance figures alone, with no map?
- Can you publish a version with the district and school removed?

## 8. Work you cannot publish

Some projects hold client or internal business information. Decide one
pattern for all of them.

- List the projects you cannot publish. One line each.
- For each one, what is the barrier? A contract, a client, or the data?
- Can you describe the method with no client name and no client data?
- Would a client approve a named case study if you asked? Which ones?
- Do you want a private case study file that you send on request?
- Should the site say that more work is available on request?
