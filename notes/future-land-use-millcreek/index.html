Future Land Use - Millcreek, Utah

A public-facing future land use map for Millcreek's General Plan, recognized as a finalist in Esri's 2025 Virtual Map Gallery.

OrganizationCity of Millcreek
Gallery2025 Esri User Conference

The Millcreek Future Land Use map translates a long-range planning policy into a visual reference that residents, developers, planners, and city staff can actually use. Instead of treating the General Plan as a document that lives on a shelf, the map gives the plan a spatial structure: where growth is encouraged, where reinvestment is likely, how corridors and centers relate to neighborhoods, and where open space and natural features remain central to the city's identity.

What the map is doing

Future land use maps sit in a tricky space. They are not parcel-level zoning maps, but they still need to be specific enough to guide real decisions. This map uses clear land-use categories, strong color grouping, roadway and boundary references, and a locator inset to make that policy geography easier to read at both citywide and neighborhood scales.

Cartographic focus

The design works to balance policy clarity with public readability. Residential, commercial, mixed-use, institutional, and open-space areas are separated with a deliberate color system, while labels, boundaries, and reference layers keep the map anchored. The legend is not just an afterthought; it is part of the composition, helping the reader connect categories to the parts of the city where those patterns matter most.

Planning value

As a General Plan product, the map supports zoning conversations, redevelopment review, housing and transportation planning, and public engagement. It also provides a transparent shared reference: the same visual can help residents understand intent, help staff explain recommendations, and help decision-makers see how individual projects fit into a broader community vision.

Recognition

Esri listed the submission as a finalist in the Envisioning the Future award category for the 2025 User Conference Virtual Map Gallery. The original gallery entry is available on Esri's site.
