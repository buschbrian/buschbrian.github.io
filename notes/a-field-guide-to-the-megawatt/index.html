A Field Guide to the Megawatt

48 Utah solar farms, each scaled to one megawatt and drawn at one ground scale. The middle value is 5.9 acres of panels per megawatt.

One megawatt, one ground scale

These outlines come from the U.S. Large-Scale Solar Photovoltaic Database (USPVDB). The U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL) publish it together.

Each farm is scaled to the footprint it would have at exactly one megawatt of alternating-current capacity, or MW AC. Every outline then uses the same ground scale. Larger shapes mean more panel area per megawatt.

The 48 farms have a median of 5.87 acres per MW AC, shown as 5.9. The range is 3.02 at SF Auto O1 to 8.96 at Three Peaks.

What the footprint counts

The database’s p_area field counts panels and inverters. It excludes fences, roads and buildings. This is not the whole site or its total disturbed area.

The analysis uses USPVDB version 4.0, dated April 14, 2026. Capacity comes from EIA Form 860. Utah has 53 database records; five are excluded:

  • Salt Palace is a rooftop array.
  • Appaloosa Solar I and Elektron have digitizing confidence 3, below the retained level of 4.
  • Castle was photographed before its 2024 operating year.
  • Red Mesa was photographed on January 12, 2023, at the start of its operating year.

The retained farms total 1,804.4 MW AC and about 11,760 acres of panels. The MW AC total describes capacity. It does not measure electricity generated, which uses units such as megawatt-hours.

Scale area without changing shape

day12_power.py projects the farm polygons into Utah-centered Albers equal-area coordinates. Acres per megawatt equals panel area in square meters, divided by 4,046.86, divided by MW AC.

To normalize a polygon to one megawatt, the script scales both axes about the centroid by the inverse square root of capacity. Scaling both axes by that factor divides area by capacity and preserves proportions.

The cover locates the farms

Beaver and Iron counties hold 28 of the 48 farms. Their 1,017.5 MW AC is 56.4% of the retained capacity.

The locator sizes dots by capacity. Its groups connect farms whose 20 km centroid buffers touch. This means each farm is within about 25 miles of another farm in its group. It does not mean every farm in a group is that close to every other farm.

The map shows where the farms cluster. The analysis does not test why they cluster there.

A field guide instead of a poster header

The title runs down a red-rock book spine. A Utah locator and three-part key sit above a six-column tray of outlines. Shapes run from most to least panel area per megawatt. Reference squares show one acre and five acres at the shapes’ scale.

Canyon places the guide on a sand page. Dusk uses a dark page and locator. Both approved finals preserve the same shapes, order and scale.

Limits

  • Mapped imagery and reported capacity can describe different dates. The exclusions reduce that mismatch but cannot remove it.
  • The footprints compare panel area, not whole-site land use.
  • The comparison uses AC capacity. It does not use DC capacity or estimate energy output.
  • The panel-type medians describe this filtered Utah set. They do not prove why the footprints differ.
