30 Day Map Challenge 2026 | Day 26: Water

Four Rivers in Relative Elevation

Four western rivers show how low ground and old channels emerge when height is measured from the river surface.

ChallengeDay 26: Water
Made withUSGS lidar and channel lines, Python, Claude for the data, maps and layouts
Challenge dateNovember 26, 2026

Use the river as zero

The land beside a river slopes downstream with it. That slope can hide low ground in an ordinary elevation map. A relative elevation model uses the river surface as the local zero. The four maps use the same height scale, from river level to 30 feet and higher. Each map has its own colors.

I sampled the river surface from 1-meter lidar along the USGS channel line. I estimated the surface between those samples, giving more weight to nearby samples, then subtracted it from the terrain. Zero represents the river surface on the day of the lidar survey.

Two places where the first method failed

Near Mosby, the mapped Musselshell channel crosses sandbars. The first estimated surface was up to about 12 feet above the bed. Using the lowest elevation in a 200-foot window brought the estimate down by about 6 feet.

The Sevier sheet stops upstream of DMAD Reservoir. The flat reservoir surface would distort the estimate for the nearby valley. Above the reservoir, the map shows low ground beside the river and an old bend downstream of Lynndyl.

Elevation from the USGS 3D Elevation Program. Channel lines from the USGS National Hydrography Dataset. I worked through the data, maps and layouts with Claude.

Build a relative elevation model

This 40-second silent animation starts with lidar terrain from the Musselshell valley. It marks the samples along the river, spreads a surface across the valley, and subtracts that surface from the terrain. Each color band on that surface marks 10 feet of fall. Bright colors trace the channel. Blues mark higher ground. The final frame shows all four maps.

Three more river maps

The Sacramento shows old channels near Ord Bend. In Montana, the Musselshell winds 27 river miles at 2.53 times its straight-line distance. The Little Snake crosses the Wyoming and Colorado line near Baggs.
