Drive Times to Utah's Five National Parks

How many of Utah's national parks can you reach by car? Move the drive time, pick your town, or select a park to see its reach.

ChallengeDay 13: Interactions
SubjectDrive times to Utah's five national parks
Made withArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript, ArcGIS Online, Python (GeoPandas, Rasterio, Matplotlib)
Challenge dateNovember 13, 2026

Half an hour changes the answer

Utah's five national parks are in the south and east of the state. Utah's largest cities are on the Wasatch Front, in the north. From Salt Lake City, the five parks are 3 h 45 min to 4 h 30 min away by car.

So a small change in drive time changes a lot. Within 4 hours, 17% of Utahns can reach all five parks. Within 4 h 30 min, 63% can. Within 5 hours, 88% can.

One park is much closer for most people. Within 3 hours, 22% of Utahns can reach at least one park. Within 4 hours, 87% can.

Each park on its own

The share of Utahns who live within 4 hours of each park:

  • Capitol Reef: 86%
  • Arches: 70%
  • Bryce Canyon: 66%
  • Canyonlands: 54%
  • Zion: 25%

Capitol Reef is near the middle of the state, so it is the easiest park to reach. Zion is the hardest. The map measures each drive to the main visitor center. For Zion, that is the Zion Canyon Visitor Center near Springdale, in the far southwest. The Kolob Canyons Visitor Center is next to I-15 and is closer for many people. This map does not use it.

Explore the map

Move the drive time to watch the reach grow. Select a park marker to map that park alone. Open the full map to type your town and read its drive time to each park.

Watch a walkthrough

How to read the map

  • The colors run from dark blue, where one park is in reach, to yellow, where all five are.
  • Uncolored land has no park within the selected time.
  • With one park selected, the colors show the drive time to that park. Bright colors are close to the park.
  • The full map's From card gives separate drive times from one town to each park. Arrows point toward each park.
  • The percentages count Utah residents. They do not change when you pick a town.

Method

  • The drive times come from one ArcGIS Online Service Area solve on November 5, 2026. It used the Driving Time travel mode toward each park, with no time of day.
  • Each park has one destination: its main visitor center. For Canyonlands, that is the Island in the Sky Visitor Center. For Zion, it is the Zion Canyon Visitor Center.
  • The solve returned simplified travel-time areas every 15 minutes, out to 5 hours. Times on the map are rounded up to the next 15 minutes.
  • For the map, the areas were drawn on a 300 m grid and smoothed. Patches smaller than 4 km² were removed.
  • The shares use 2020 Census blocks. Each block counts at its internal point. The shares use the areas before smoothing.
  • The map uses an Albers equal-area projection centered on Utah. The relief is a hillshade of U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) elevation at 400 m.

Inside the scripts

A Python script, day13_reach.py, turns the drive-time areas into the shares on this page with GeoPandas and pandas. The map runs in the browser on the ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript.

The Python script passes the default checks of the ruff linter and uses its code format. The excerpts below are trimmed. A # ... or // ... line marks a cut.

Each Census block counts at one point inside it. The script puts the points and the drive-time areas in the same coordinate system, then joins them. A spatial join finds every 15-minute ring that a point falls inside. The smallest ring is that block's drive time to the park. Then one comparison and a sum across each row count the parks within any drive time.

The slider doesn't ask a server for anything. Each map layer already holds every drive time, from 15 minutes to 5 hours. When the slider moves, the page sets a filter on the layer's view, and the map redraws only the shapes that match.

Data notes

  • The times are modeled. Real trips change with traffic, weather, road work and stops.
  • The shares count Utah residents only. People in nearby states, such as in Las Vegas or Grand Junction, are not counted.
  • The roads on the map are in Utah only.
  • A drive to the visitor center is not a drive to the park boundary. Some park land is closer than its visitor center.

Data and credit

Esri's ArcGIS Online routing service solved the drive times. The U.S. Census Bureau provides the 2020 population blocks. The National Park Service (NPS) Land Resources Division provides the park boundaries. OpenStreetMap contributors provide the roads, water, towns and visitor center locations, under the Open Database License. USGS 3DEP provides the elevation. The Utah Geospatial Resource Center (UGRC) provides the Utah boundary.
