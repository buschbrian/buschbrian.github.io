A 10-Minute Walk to Transit on the Wasatch Front

Using 2020 Census counts, the model places more than half of residents within a 10-minute walk of a scheduled stop. About 21% live that close to frequent service at weekday midday.

ChallengeDay 7: 10 minute map
SubjectWalking access to transit in Weber, Davis, Salt Lake and Utah counties
Made withArcGIS Pro (Network Analyst), Python (GeoPandas, Matplotlib)
Challenge dateNovember 7, 2026

Near a stop, and near frequent service

This map measures a 10-minute walk to transit along streets and paths. It covers Weber, Davis, Salt Lake and Utah counties, from Ogden to Provo.

The model combines 2020 Census counts with the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) schedule for Thursday, November 5, 2026. It estimates that 1.35 million people, 55% of the four counties’ residents, are within a 10-minute walk of a scheduled stop.

About 530,000 people, 21%, are that close to service scheduled every 15 minutes or better at weekday midday. About 92,000 people, 4%, are that close to service scheduled every 10 minutes or better in the same window.

The 10-minute tier runs along three corridors: the TRAX trunk in Salt Lake City, OGX in Ogden, and UVX in Provo and Orem. This is an independent analysis of UTA's published schedule.

For the “10 minute map” theme, 10 minutes describes the modeled walk. The analysis and map took longer to make.

What counts as frequent

The General Transit Feed Specification (GTFS) is the standard format for published transit schedules. UTA publishes its schedule in that format. The first script, day07_frequency.py, reads the GTFS text files with the Python standard library only. The scripts pass the default checks of the ruff linter and use its code format. The excerpts below are trimmed. A # ... line marks a cut.

  • A stop is frequent when its departures leave no gap over 15 minutes between 11:00 and 14:00. This cutoff follows the frequency in UTA’s frequent-service network. This map checks weekday midday only.
  • The map shows a second tier, with no gap over 10 minutes, in its own color.
  • The count includes the last departure before that window and the first one after it.
  • The script counts a departure where boarding is allowed and the trip continues to another stop.

Each TRAX line runs every 15 minutes on its own. Where lines share track, a rider sees a train more often. So the script counts departures toward the same next stop together, across all routes. That rule splits where lines diverge, so some branch stations count in one direction only.

At 15 minutes, each route alone gives 1,010 frequent stops, and departures combined toward the same next stop give 1,022. The grouping matters little at 15 minutes. It matters at 10: each route alone gives 44 stops, all on OGX and UVX, and combined departures give 73, including 25 TRAX platforms on the shared trunk.

The first version of this map used 10 minutes. That line is a sharp edge: 13 of the 25 TRAX platforms sit at exactly 10.0 minutes. The final map uses a 15-minute cutoff at weekday midday and keeps the 10-minute tier as its own color.

Walking on the street network

The walk network comes from OpenStreetMap (OSM), a map that volunteers build. day07_walk_network.py reads the OSM extract and keeps the ways a person can walk on: sidewalks, paths, streets and plazas.

  • It drops freeways and their ramps, and any way tagged foot=no or access=no.
  • It keeps private roads, including apartment and business-park roads. Their mapped access rules may be incomplete.
  • It splits each way only at nodes it shares with another kept way. A sidewalk on a bridge does not join the road below it.
  • OSM leaves some TRAX platforms unconnected to any walkway. Platforms stay out of the network, and rail stops snap to the nearest walkway.

ArcGIS Pro Network Analyst then builds a network dataset on length. A Service Area is the part of a network within a set distance of a point, here the walkshed of each stop. The script solves Service Areas out to 800 m, a 10-minute walk at 4.8 km/h.

The regional network has 1,148,031 edges from 464,460 OSM ways. All 5,402 scheduled stops snap to it within 100 m. The median snap is 2.5 m.

Counting residents

Residents come from 2020 Census blocks. A Census block is the smallest area the Census Bureau counts, often one city block. The four counties recorded 2,469,539 residents in the 2020 Census.

day07_population.py solves one dissolved 800 m Service Area polygon for all stops and one for frequent stops. It intersects each polygon with the blocks. Then it spreads each block's residents evenly over the block's area.

Checks

  • ArcGIS Pro's Calculate Transit Service Frequency tool gives the same departure counts at every stop when it counts departures the same way. Its longest gaps match the script's to within 0.1 minute.
  • Counting whole blocks by their internal point gives 53.0% near any stop. "More than half" holds under every tested method.
  • For the 10-minute tier, splitting edge blocks by OSM building footprint instead of land area gives 91,097 people, against 92,187.
  • In the 10-minute run, a 50 m polygon trim moved each share by half a point at most.
  • In a test area around Murray Central, walksheds cross I-15 only at the Vine Street overpass. They cross the Jordan River only on bridges.
  • Across the region, no walkway crosses a freeway at grade in the network.

Symbol guide

  • Gold streets are within a 10-minute walk of any bus or train stop.
  • Navy to pale teal streets are within 10 minutes of a stop with service scheduled every 15 minutes or better. Darker means a shorter walk.
  • Dark to pale rose streets are within 10 minutes of a stop with service scheduled every 10 minutes or better.
  • Large dots mark stops in the 10-minute tier. Small dots mark the other frequent stops.
  • Faint gray lines are the other streets and paths.
  • The tall overview shows the four counties. Three street panels at one scale show the TRAX trunk, OGX and UVX.
  • The resident counts on the street panels are county totals near frequent service.

Data notes

  • The model uses the November 5, 2026 service day from UTA’s October 8, 2026 GTFS feed. These are scheduled departures, not observed service.
  • "Every 15 minutes or better" and "every 10 minutes or better" cover 11:00 to 14:00 on that weekday only.
  • The walk cutoff is 800 m, equivalent to 10 minutes at 4.8 km/h. Hills and waits at signals are not in the model.
  • Some FrontRunner stops sit on platforms between tracks. They snap to the nearest walkway, which can understate the walk by about a minute. FrontRunner is not frequent at either tier, so the frequent shares do not change.
  • Stops outside the four counties stay in the network. Their walksheds count only where they reach four-county blocks.
  • This analysis does not speak for UTA.

Data and credit
