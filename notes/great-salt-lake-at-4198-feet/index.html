30 Day Map Challenge 2026 | Day 8: Utopia

Great Salt Lake at 4,198 Feet

The USGS model of Great Salt Lake, flooded to a higher shoreline. At 4,198 feet, the lake would cover about half again as much area as on November 3.

SubjectGreat Salt Lake at the low end of the Strike Team’s healthy range
Made withPython (Rasterio, SciPy, Matplotlib), GDAL
Challenge dateNovember 8, 2026

Eight feet higher

Utopia is the theme for Day 8. This map draws Great Salt Lake at 4,198 feet. That level is the low end of the 4,198 to 4,205 foot range that the Great Salt Lake Strike Team calls healthy.

On November 3, 2026, the USGS gauge at Saltair on the South Arm read 4,190.0 feet. This value is provisional. At 4,198 feet, the lake would cover about 3,625 km², up from 2,398 km². That is about 1,227 km² (474 square miles) more, a 51% gain. The lake would hold about 7.0 km³ more water, or 5.7 million acre-feet.

At that level, Farmington Bay and Bear River Bay hold water again. Spiral Jetty sits about 2 feet under water. The map shows a shoreline at a stated elevation. It is not a forecast.

Two datums for one lake

A vertical datum is the zero point that a set of elevations is measured from. The lake gauges and the lake model use different datums.

  • The USGS gauges report lake levels in the National Geodetic Vertical Datum of 1929 (NGVD 29).
  • The USGS lake model uses the North American Vertical Datum of 1988 (NAVD 88).
  • The Strike Team’s report draws its range on the USGS gauge record. So this map reads 4,198 feet in NGVD 29.

The USGS elevation-area-volume tables give an offset for each part of the lake. NAVD 88 is 3.48 feet higher than NGVD 29 for the South Arm and both bays. It is 3.06 feet higher for the North Arm. The flood uses these offsets because it is checked against the same tables.

VERTCON is the standard conversion between the two datums. At four points on the lake, it gives offsets of 2.97 to 3.30 feet. These values are up to about 0.4 feet below the South Arm table. That gap is the uncertainty in the conversion.

The model’s metadata says that its values are in meters. They are in feet. The values run from 4,170.09 to 6,654.81, and the tables start at 4,170 feet.

The two arms

A railroad causeway splits the lake into a North Arm and a South Arm. On November 3, the North Arm gauge near Saline read 4,189.7 feet. That is 0.3 feet below the South Arm.

The map keeps that gap. When the South Arm stands at 4,198 feet, the North Arm stands at 4,197.7 feet. Farmington Bay and Bear River Bay follow the South Arm.

Flooding the model

The base is the USGS half-meter topobathymetric model of Great Salt Lake. A topobathymetric model joins land elevation and lake-bottom depth in one surface. This model merges 2005–06 bathymetry with 2016 lidar.

The script day08_flood_gate.py resamples the model to 10 m cells. Then it floods each part of the lake from its own seed.

  • Each part of the lake is the largest connected area below its level inside a search box. Cells connect only through a shared edge, not a corner.
  • OpenStreetMap (OSM) rail lines, causeways and dikes act as walls.
  • The 657 salt ponds and diked water bodies in OSM cannot fill.
  • The script measures area and volume from the flooded cells.

The first run walled off Bear River Bay behind the railroad crossing to Little Mountain. The South Arm came up 4.8% short of its table. The low ground behind that crossing measured exactly the table’s 85.4 km² for Bear River Bay. So the crossing is the edge of the bay in the USGS tables, not a wall. Now the bay floods from its own seed at the South Arm level.

Where the shore moves

The map labels five places. The script day08_anchors.py measures the straight-line distance from each place to the nearest flooded cell. On 10 m cells, the distances are good to about 10 m.

  • Spiral Jetty: water 0.6 km away on November 3. Under water at 4,198 feet.
  • Promontory Point: water 0.6 km away on November 3, and 0.1 km at 4,198 feet.
  • Bridger Bay: water 1.6 km out on November 3. Back to the beach at 4,198 feet.
  • Saltair: water 0.9 km away on November 3, and 0.2 km at 4,198 feet.
  • Great Salt Lake Marina: the open lake is 0.4 km off the harbor on November 3. It reaches the harbor at 4,198 feet. The 2016 lidar cannot show whether the dredged harbor holds water, so the map measures only the open lake.

The height of Spiral Jetty comes from the half-meter model, inside its OSM outline. The median height of its crest is 4,195.4 feet, read on the North Arm gauge. At 4,198 feet, 98.9% of the jetty is under water, about 2.3 feet over its median crest. The jetty goes under at every offset in the VERTCON range.

Symbol guide

  • Light blue is the lake on November 3, 2026.
  • Purple is the water that comes back at 4,198 feet. Its color blends into the light blue at the November 3 shore.
  • Darker waterlines step in from the 4,198-foot shore through both colors.
  • A very faint wash shows ground up to 4,205 feet, the top of the Strike Team’s range.
  • Tan patches are diked mineral ponds. The flood keeps them dry.
  • The thin white line across the middle is the railroad causeway between the two arms.
  • Large figures give the gain in area and volume, and the rise at Saltair.
  • A small map in the corner places the lake among the western states.

Data notes

  • The lake levels are USGS daily values for November 3, 2026. They are provisional, and USGS can revise them.
  • The lake-bottom survey is from 2005–06. The land elevation is from 2016 lidar.
  • Areas and volumes come from the flood. The flood keeps the diked ponds dry.
  • The map shows a shoreline at a stated elevation. It does not show how or when the lake could reach that level.
  • This analysis does not speak for USGS or the Great Salt Lake Strike Team.

Data and credit

The U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) publishes the half-meter lake model and its elevation-area-volume tables (Root, 2023). The lake levels come from USGS gauges 10010000 at Saltair Boat Harbor and 10010100 near Saline. The healthy range is from the Great Salt Lake Strike Team. The causeways, dikes and ponds come from OpenStreetMap. The locator map uses U.S. Census Bureau state boundaries. Python and GDAL make the map.
