Drought in 75 Western Basins

The U.S. Drought Monitor draws its map by hand each week. Here its polygons meet the drainage basins of the West.

The polygons are the data

The U.S. Drought Monitor is a weekly map. Its authors draw the drought lines by hand. They combine many indicators with local reports. D0 means abnormally dry conditions. Drought begins at D1, moderate drought, and extends to D4, exceptional drought.

The map here clips those polygons to 75 drainage basins. A drainage basin is land that drains toward a common outlet. These are six-digit hydrologic units, called HUC-6 basins, from the USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset.

On October 27, 21 of the 75 basins had some land in extreme or exceptional drought, D3 or D4.

Ten years, basin by basin

The second scene changes the measure. Each basin gets the share of its land at D2, severe drought, or worse. The scene replays the available weekly maps from November 2016 through October 2026.

This measure shows how much of each basin is affected. A small D4 patch does not set the color for the whole basin.

Across all 75 basins, the highest share in this record was 88%, on July 27, 2021. The combined share weights each basin by its U.S. land area.

Less land in severe drought this fall

In 2026 the share reached 47% on August 4. By October 27 it was 23%.

From October 20 to October 27, 19 basins had less land at D2 or worse. None had more. The other 56 did not change.

Some basins did not move much. The Jordan basin in Utah is at 98%. Middle Snake-Powder, on the Oregon and Idaho border, is at 96%. Fifteen basins are at 50% or more.

Symbol guide

  • Thin outlines mark the HUC-6 basins. Basins that cross into Canada show only their U.S. land in color.
  • The first scene uses the Drought Monitor classes, from pale yellow for D0 to dark red for D4.
  • The other scenes use six classes for the share of land at D2 or worse, from none to 75% or more.
  • All maps use an equal-area projection, so basin sizes compare fairly.

Method

  • The drought polygons come from the U.S. Drought Monitor, through a fixed snapshot of the Western Water Dashboard’s drought data.
  • The basins are the 75 HUC-6 units in hydrologic regions 14 through 18, from the USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset. Only U.S. land is colored.
  • The maps use the Contiguous USA Albers equal-area projection (EPSG:5070), so basin areas compare fairly.
  • Each basin’s value is its share of land at D2 or worse. The combined share weights each basin by its U.S. land area.
  • The data snapshot lacks February 17 and 24, 2026. The animation skips those dates.

Data and credit

The National Drought Mitigation Center, USDA and NOAA produce the U.S. Drought Monitor. USGS provides the Watershed Boundary Dataset. The state lines come from Natural Earth. The Western Water Dashboard measures the share of each basin in each class.
