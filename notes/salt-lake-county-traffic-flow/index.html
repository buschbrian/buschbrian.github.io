Traffic Flow on Salt Lake County's State Highways

A map of how busy the state highways of Salt Lake County were in 2025. The wider the line, the more traffic the road carries.

OccasionGIS Day
Subject2025 traffic on state highways in Salt Lake County, Utah
Made withPython (GeoPandas, Shapely, rasterio, Matplotlib), FFmpeg
PublishedNovember 18, 2026

It started at the Esri User Conference

GIS Day is on November 18 this year. GIS means geographic information system. To mark the day, here is a map I wanted to make for a long time.

At the 2025 Esri User Conference, I saw a traffic flow map of San Diego County by Caltrans, the California Department of Transportation. The width of each road showed how busy it was, and a small number sat beside it. I liked it a lot. I wanted one for Salt Lake County, in a desert orange.

One orange

The roads, the lake and the relief are all the same desert orange, in lighter and darker mixes. The title and the place names are a dark brown.

The width of a road shows its traffic. There are six widths, from under 10,000 vehicles a day to more than 200,000. I-15 is the heaviest line on the map. The canyon roads are some of the lightest. On the wider roads, a thin white line runs down the middle, as on the San Diego map.

The numbers are in a handwritten style. Each one sits beside a white dot on its road. The map has 430 of these road sections, and 346 of the numbers fit.

The lake and the mountains

Great Salt Lake is a stack of lines that follow the shore out into the water. The shore is the lake at 4,205 feet, the top of its healthy range. I used the same lake model for Great Salt Lake at 4,198 feet.

Antelope Island has its name along its full length. I gave it more relief than the rest of the map, so that it stands up out of the lake.

Faint shaded relief shows the Oquirrh Mountains and the Wasatch Range. The names of Parleys, Big Cottonwood and Little Cottonwood canyons follow their roads in pale letters. Outside the county, the map fades to half strength.

The small things

  • Route markers name the highways. Utah's state route markers have a beehive on them.
  • Signs at the edges of the map point to the next places down the road, such as Provo and Utah County to the south. They use the letters of a highway sign.
  • The Utah locator at the bottom has a beehive and the word UTAH cut out of it. The beehive follows the one on the 2024 Utah state flag. The relief shows through the cutout.
  • An inset map shows the Wasatch Front, from Brigham City south past Provo.

About the numbers

Each number is the annual average daily traffic (AADT) for one road section. That is the traffic for a year, divided by 365. The values come from Utah's 2025 report to the Federal Highway Administration, rounded to the nearest hundred. The busiest section is I-15 at the I-80 interchange in South Salt Lake, at 342,800 vehicles a day.

My first try in ArcGIS Pro had gaps at the interchanges, because its traffic data had few ramps. The 2025 data has the ramps, so the roads now connect.

Data and credit

  • Traffic: Federal Highway Administration, Highway Performance Monitoring System, Utah, data year 2025. The Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) submits these data.
  • Shaded relief: USGS 3D Elevation Program, 5 m.
  • Great Salt Lake: the USGS topobathymetric model at 4,205 feet, with causeways and dikes from OpenStreetMap contributors.
  • Utah Lake, roads, counties and city boundaries: the Utah Geospatial Resource Center (UGRC). Utah Lake comes from the USGS National Hydrography Dataset through UGRC.
  • Route markers: Wikimedia Commons, public domain. I made some of the Utah markers from their digits.
