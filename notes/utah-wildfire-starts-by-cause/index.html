Utah Wildfire Starts by Cause

Natural and human-caused wildfire starts in Utah, mapped apart and then together. Human-caused starts cluster more tightly.

ChallengeDay 4: Clusters
SubjectWildfire starts in Utah, 1992–2024
Made withPython (GeoPandas, SciPy, Matplotlib), FFmpeg, ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript
Challenge dateNovember 4, 2026

Recorded wildfire starts

The Fire Program Analysis fire-occurrence database (FPA FOD) records wildfire starts across the United States. Its 7th edition covers 1992 to 2024. Inside Utah's boundary it holds 41,117 records.

Natural causes account for 54% of those records. People caused 38%. The other 8% have no cause recorded, so neither panel shows them. Across 75 western drainage basins, natural causes account for 32% of starts. Utah reverses the western pattern.

The source's Natural category includes more than lightning.

From dots to density

Dots stack up where starts are common. Density smoothing spreads each recorded start over nearby land. Nearby starts contribute most. The stills use a Gaussian kernel with a 5 km standard deviation, calculated on a 1 km grid.

Both causes use one log scale, in starts per 1,000 km² per year. The colors compare directly across the two panels.

Each cause has its own densest tenth of Utah's land. That tenth holds 60% of human-caused starts and 46% of natural starts. Human-caused starts follow the Wasatch Front, I-15 and towns. Natural starts spread over the plateaus and mountains.

Where they meet

The last scene blends both surfaces on one map. Yellow marks dense natural starts. Red marks dense human-caused starts. Pale peach marks land where both are dense.

Both surfaces reach 10 starts per 1,000 km² per year on 633 km², 0.3% of Utah. Natural starts alone reach it on 6.0% of the state. Human-caused starts alone reach it on 4.4%.

62% of natural starts land where human-caused starts are rare, under 3 per 1,000 km² per year. July and August hold 71% of natural starts and 41% of human-caused starts.

Explore the starts

The interactive map draws the same 37,882 starts with a known cause. Filter them by cause and by year. Show them as clusters, as a density surface, or as single dots.

Symbol guide

  • Yellow is natural causes. Red is human causes.
  • The density ramps run from dark at 3 starts per 1,000 km² per year to pale at 80 or more.
  • The two-way key on the combined map reads natural density across and human density up.
  • White outlines mark land where both surfaces reach 10 starts per 1,000 km² per year.
  • The basemap is shaded relief with lakes, highways and town labels.

Method

  • The fire records are FPA FOD, 7th edition, clipped to the Utah Geospatial Resource Center (UGRC) Utah boundary.
  • The cause groups use the database's cause classification: natural, human, or missing.
  • The maps use an Albers equal-area projection centered on Utah, so areas compare fairly.
  • Density uses a Gaussian kernel with a 5 km standard deviation on a 1 km grid, divided by the 33 years of record.
  • Each cause's densest tenth is measured on its own surface. The two tenths are different areas.
  • The relief is a multi-direction hillshade from Amazon Web Services (AWS) Terrain Tiles at zoom 9.
  • The interactive map uses a different kernel with a radius of about 14 km. It is calibrated to approximate the stills. The two methods can produce different local values.

Data notes

  • The 41,117 total is a spatial clip to Utah. The database's own state field gives 41,133.
  • Starts outside Utah are not in the data, so density near the state line reads low.
  • The maps count starts. They do not show acres burned or future fire risk.

Data and credit
