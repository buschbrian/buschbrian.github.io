30 Day Map Challenge 2026 | Day 28: Feeling

Exposure Around Moab

Five cliff edges near Moab show how far the ground drops within 164 feet of where a person can stand.

ChallengeDay 28: Feeling
Made withUSGS lidar, Blender, Python, Claude for the data, map and layout, GPT-6.2 Astra for the final renders
Challenge dateNovember 28, 2026

Map the drop

The feeling many people come to Moab for is exposure: the ground falls away close to your feet. This map measures that drop.

For each place on the ground, I found the lowest ground within 164 feet (50 meters). The drop is the difference in height. The map glows where that drop is 164 feet or more. The overview uses a USGS elevation model with cells about 10 meters across. Cells that size soften sheer walls.

Count only ground you can stand on

For the five edges, I used USGS lidar with 1-meter cells. My first renders lit whole cliff faces. At 1 meter, every cell on a wall has a large drop beside it. I then counted only ground with a slope under about 35 degrees. Only the rims and ledges stayed lit.

Each figure is the largest drop from that ground within 330 feet (100 meters) of the overlook. For Parriott Mesa, I searched within 0.6 miles (1 kilometer) of the named summit.

  • Colorado River Overlook, BLM land: 726 feet
  • Dead Horse Point, Utah State Parks: 653 feet
  • Parriott Mesa, BLM land: 595 feet
  • Green River Overlook, Canyonlands National Park: 581 feet
  • Mineral Bottom, BLM land: 541 feet

Where BASE jumping is legal

The largest drop on the overview is 866 feet, near the Green River Overlook. That rim is in Canyonlands National Park. The park prohibits BASE jumping.

BASE jumping is legal on most of the BLM land around Moab, according to the local group Moab BASE Access. The group asks jumpers to stay out of Mineral and Hell Roaring canyons to protect wildlife. The BLM news release of January 30, 2023 names the Mineral Bottom BASE Jumping Focus Area. GearJunkie names Parriott Mesa as one of three cliffs for tandem jumps. The map does not mark exit points.

What is data and what is illustration

The terrain and the glow come from data. Each render shows lidar terrain at true scale, with no vertical exaggeration. A terrain model with 30-meter cells fills the distance.

Some parts are illustration:

  • Rock color and texture
  • The sunset light and sky
  • The haze in the distance

I sampled the rock colors from one of my own photographs of the area. The distant glow is dimmed so the near rim reads first.

Five edges

Three edges are overlooks. Two are BASE jumping areas on BLM land. Each render looks back at the rim from out over the drop.

Fly to the edge

This 13-second silent flyover moves toward the Colorado River Overlook at sunset. It ends over the glowing rim, where the ground drops 726 feet within 164 feet.

Lidar and elevation from the USGS 3D Elevation Program. Park boundaries from the Utah Geospatial Resource Center land ownership layer. Overlook points from OpenStreetMap contributors. Place names from the USGS Geographic Names Information System. Rendered in Blender. I worked through the data, map and layout with Claude, and the final renders with GPT-6.2 Astra.
