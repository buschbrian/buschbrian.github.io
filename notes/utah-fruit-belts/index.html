30 Day Map Challenge 2026 | Day 23: Taste

Utah's Fruit Belts

Most of Utah's orchards grow in two belts. One is around Santaquin and Genola in Utah County. The other is the Fruit Way below Brigham City. A third map shows the historic orchards at Fruita in Capitol Reef National Park.

Two counties have most of the orchards

The 2022 Census of Agriculture counts 7,445 acres of orchards in Utah. Utah County has 5,226 acres. Box Elder County has 919 acres. Together, the two counties have 82 percent of the state total.

Cherries are the largest crop:

  • Tart cherries: 3,714 acres
  • Sweet cherries: 285 acres
  • Peaches: 1,310 acres
  • Apples: 1,104 acres

The census counts only farms. It does not count the orchards in a national park, such as Fruita.

The maps show fields

The fields come from the Water-Related Land Use data of the Utah Division of Water Resources, 2025 edition. Each field has a crop class. Each fruit has its own color. Purple is an orchard with mixed fruit, or an orchard with no named fruit.

On the two belt maps, I merged the fields of one fruit that touch. Then I removed the merged areas smaller than 1 acre. These two steps remove small marks, and the large blocks stay easy to read.

The south Utah County map shows the cherry blocks around Santaquin and Genola. The Fruit Way map shows the peach fields along US-89 from Willard to Brigham City.

Fruita, orchard by orchard

Fruita is a historic orchard district in Capitol Reef National Park. The land use data gives no fruit for 18 of the 21 fields in my Fruita frame.

The National Park Service gives each orchard a number on its brochure map. I matched each field to an orchard on that map. To place the fields, I used the roads, Sulphur Creek and the Fremont River on 1 m (3 ft) aerial imagery from the National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP). 14 of the 20 matches are clear. Six are probable.

Each orchard has the main fruit from a 2005 tree inventory by Routson and Nabhan at Northern Arizona University. By that count, apples lead by a small margin:

  • Apples: 740 trees
  • Peaches: 717 trees
  • Apricots: 542 trees

The park has planted new trees since 2005. The colors show the 2005 inventory.

The land use data has no field for the south part of the Mott orchard. I drew that part from the NAIP imagery.

The season chart

The chart shows when each fruit is in season along the Wasatch Front, by half month. It comes from a 2016 fact sheet by USU Extension. Sweet cherries have a darker red than tart cherries.

Method

  • The fields are the orchard classes of the Water-Related Land Use data, 2025 edition. I left out the berry fields: 131 acres in the state data, and less than 5 acres on each belt map.
  • I left out two round fields west of Utah Lake. Each is 127 acres, and the imagery shows center pivots.
  • The acres in the text and on the fruit plates come from the 2022 Census of Agriculture, from the USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service. They are not sums of the fields on the maps.
  • The aerial imagery is NAIP from the U.S. Department of Agriculture, served by the U.S. Geological Survey. The belt maps use an 8 m or 10 m version. Fruita uses 1 m.
  • The fruit stands come from the agritourism and Utah's Own directories of the Utah Department of Agriculture and Food. The town names use the municipal boundaries of the Utah Geospatial Resource Center (UGRC).
  • Each map has its own scale. All three maps are north up.
  • Codex image generation made the three engraved fruit plates. The scripts set every word and number.

Limits

  • The census and the land use data measure different things. The census counts farm acres in 2022. The land use data maps fields in 2025.
  • Six of the 20 Fruita matches are probable, not certain. The brochure map is a schematic.
  • The Mott orchard shows 7.0 acres on the map. The 2005 inventory gives 9.7 acres.
  • The fruit stands are only the stands in the two state directories. Other stands are not on the map.
  • Apples and apricots can look alike to a person with red-green color blindness. The key and the Fruita table give a name to each color.

Data and credit

The Utah Division of Water Resources provides the Water-Related Land Use data. The USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service provides the Census of Agriculture. The National Park Service provides the Fruita brochure map. Routson and Nabhan wrote the 2007 orchard inventory at Northern Arizona University. USU Extension provides the season chart. USDA NAIP, served by USGS, provides the imagery. UGRC provides the town boundaries. The Utah Department of Agriculture and Food provides the stand directories.
