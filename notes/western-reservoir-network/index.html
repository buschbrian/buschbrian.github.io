30 Day Map Challenge 2026 | Day 2: Lines

How the West’s Reservoirs Connect

An inferred reservoir network, drawn over the dashboard’s ArcGIS map.

Challenge30 Day Map Challenge 2026, Day 2: Lines
SubjectReservoir networks
ToolsArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript, Python, FFmpeg
PublishedNovember 2, 2026

The U.S. Geological Survey’s Network-Linked Data Index traces land that drains to each reservoir. I used those traces to infer connections among the reservoirs in the dataset.

For each reservoir, I selected the smallest downstream basin whose trace includes it. This identifies the next represented reservoir downstream. Reservoirs outside the dataset can sit between linked points.

SNOTEL is a mountain monitoring network run by the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service. Each snow station links to the smallest traced reservoir basin that contains it.

The map has 256 reservoir links across 400 traced reservoirs. It has 44 connected groups with two or more reservoirs. It also links 488 snow stations.

I added the inferred links to the dashboard’s ArcGIS map for this figure. The animation highlights a chain of 12 reservoirs. It starts at Jackson Lake and ends at Lake Celilo on the Columbia River. The straight links do not follow river paths or show managed water transfers.

The Western Water Dashboard lists reservoirs upstream of a selected reservoir. It also links to a view of upstream snow stations. The dashboard is built on Esri’s ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript.
