30 Day Map Challenge 2026 | Day 27: New tool

Shading the San Rafael Swell

Eight tests with Eduard Cloud show how nearby terrain changes the shaded relief of the San Rafael Swell.

ChallengeDay 27: New tool
Made withEduard Cloud, GDAL, Claude for the data, map and layout
Challenge dateNovember 27, 2026

Shade the same terrain two ways

I used one USGS elevation model for a GDAL multidirectional hillshade and eight tests in Eduard Cloud. The grid has cells about 10 meters across. Eduard learned to shade terrain from examples of hand-drawn relief. The tool credits Monash University and ETH Zurich.

In this comparison, Eduard kept more of the small canyons and valley floors visible along the San Rafael Reef. I judged this by looking at the two images in the same area.

The shading frame changed the contrast

The elevation model reaches 10,578 feet at the edge of the Wasatch Plateau. Aerial perspective in Eduard lowers the contrast of low terrain, so high terrain stands out. In the tests with aerial perspective at 75 and 100, much of the Swell faded beside the higher terrain. I kept the plateau in the shading frame. The finished map shows it in the northwest corner.

The final map uses these Eduard settings:

  • Aerial perspective 20
  • Contrast 70
  • Light turned 30 degrees west
  • The Large shading style

Read the elevations

Color shows elevation, from 4,132 feet in the desert to 10,578 feet on the plateau. Three summits show their height:

  • Head of Sinbad, 7,129 feet
  • Temple Mountain, 6,759 feet
  • Mexican Mountain, 6,389 feet

For each summit, I used the highest elevation within 200 meters of the named USGS point.

Terrain from the USGS 3D Elevation Program. Place names from the USGS Geographic Names Information System. Relief drawn in Eduard. I worked through the data, map and layout with Claude.
