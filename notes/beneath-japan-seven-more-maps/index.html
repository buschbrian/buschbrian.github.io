One Prompt, Seven More Maps of Japan

After the challenge day, I gave the same brief to seven more models from four companies. All seven finished within the 30-minute limit, and every number I could recompute held up. Three posters put the deepest earthquake in the wrong place, and the Gemini posters have the smallest text yet.

The same brief, seven more times

Part 1 gave one brief to six models on November 5 and 6. Each model had 30 minutes to make a finished earthquake poster of Japan, with no follow-up from me. Part 1 has the full prompt, the first six maps, and three sets of scores.

This page follows the same brief through seven more runs:

  • November 9: Claude Fable 5.2, on the day of its release, and Grok 4.7.
  • November 22: GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol in Codex Desktop.
  • November 22: Gemini 4 Neon, Gemini 4 Argon, and Gemini 4 Hydrogen in Antigravity, Google’s agent app.

Each session had one human message: the prompt, unchanged. You can download the prompt. The models could inspect and revise their own work within the limit.

As in Part 1, most tokens are context that the model reads again on each request. The provider caches that context and bills it at a lower rate. Output tokens include the models’ reasoning.

The same work can cost very different amounts:

  • Astra and Sol used almost the same tokens: 32 requests each and about 31,000 output tokens. Astra lists at four times Sol’s price for each token, so its run cost four times as much.
  • Hydrogen used the most tokens, 6.37 million, on Extra High. It has the lowest prices of the three Gemini models, so it cost less than Neon.
  • Fable made only 13 requests, because it runs many tool calls in each one. About half its cost is cache writes: its app keeps context in a one-hour cache, which bills writes at twice the input price.

Some notes on the prices:

  • No request reached a price threshold for long prompts. Grok bills a request at double rates when its prompt reaches 200,000 tokens. OpenAI’s threshold is 272,000 tokens. Anthropic and Google have none for these models.
  • The Grok app logged its model as “grok-4.7-build”, which is not on xAI’s price list. I used the prices for grok-4.7. The app’s own cost estimate, $2.91, agrees within one cent.
  • Gemini 4 Argon’s list prices double on December 1, 2026. The same run would then cost about $3.30.
  • Codex Desktop also ran an automatic approval reviewer, a separate model that checks some actions before they run. It used 116,131 more tokens. The table does not include them.

These figures do not compare the models fairly. The apps, effort settings, and context differed. The prices are from the Anthropic, xAI, OpenAI, and Google pricing pages.

The brief required a 540 by 675 pixel preview and asked each model to check it. These are the seven previews, unchanged, shown near phone width. The first four have body text near 8 px at this size, larger than any poster in Part 1. The three Gemini posters have body text of 3 to 4 px.

What the checks supported

I checked each run against its transcript and its saved files. For the five November 22 runs, I recomputed each printed number from the run’s own data and rendered each poster again from its own script.

  • All seven runs used the same 515 earthquake records as the six runs in Part 1.
  • Every number I could recompute matches the saved data. That includes Argon’s rank correlation of −0.61 and the Tokara swarm counts on Neon and Hydrogen.
  • Three posters from two companies chose the same main finding. On Astra, Sol, and Neon, all 116 earthquakes in the 36–40°N band at or east of 142°E were shallower than 70 km.
  • The five November 22 folders render their posters again pixel for pixel. Argon does so only with a Python environment outside its folder. The environment it left in the folder is empty.

Argon also prints “two offshore belts each produced 144 events”. The script types that figure as text instead of computing it. Reasonable boundaries for the two belts give 139 to 147 earthquakes, so I could not confirm it exactly.

While it looked for map data, Argon searched my home folder. The results listed the data files from the Part 1 runs. Argon did not open or copy them.

The issues that remained

The deepest earthquake, placed three wrong ways. The deepest earthquake of 2025 in this area was 561 km down, on October 25. USGS names its place “40 km NW of Slavyanka, Russia”. That name gives the nearest town. By the Natural Earth 1:10m border, the epicenter itself is about 9 km inside China and about 41 km from the nearest coast. Three posters misplace it:

  • Fable 5.2: “beneath Russia’s coast”.
  • GPT-6.2 Astra: “beneath mainland Russia”, on the poster and in its documentation.
  • Gemini 4 Argon: “Sea of Japan”, in its header card and its first annotation.

Grok repeats the USGS place name. Sol, Neon, and Hydrogen label the point without naming a place. Nothing in the catalog record was wrong. Each error came from a sentence a model wrote around a correct point.

Text too small for a phone, on the Gemini posters. Fable, Grok, Astra, and Sol kept their main text near 8 px at the required preview size. The three Gemini posters put far more text on the page and set it at 3 to 4 px. Only their titles and largest numbers read at phone size.

An interpretation stated as fact. Astra’s depth-plot annotation says the deep events lie where “the Pacific plate sinks beneath Honshu”. The brief asked each poster to separate observations from interpretation. Astra’s documentation does separate them and cites USGS. Sol, Neon, Argon, and Hydrogen label their interpretations as interpretations.

The order of the depth colors. Fable, Grok, Sol, Neon, and Hydrogen run from light at the surface to dark at depth, the usual order. Astra and Argon reverse it, so their deepest earthquakes are the lightest or brightest marks.

Portability. Neon and Argon write their own folder path into their scripts, so a copy elsewhere needs one edit. Argon also depends on a Python environment outside its folder. Astra, Sol, and Hydrogen rebuild from their folders with the packages their documentation lists.

Smaller gaps. The legends on Astra and Sol give no counts. Astra’s seafloor tints have no legend on the poster, so a reader could take them for earthquake depth. Astra’s band shading is faint. Pale symbols overlap in the dense clusters on Sol.

Why Grok’s ocean is cream

The Grok script meant to draw navy water and cream land. The small locator map shows those colors. On the main map, the water is cream.

The script keeps each Natural Earth land record that touches the map area. One record also holds Antarctica. The poster uses a Lambert conic projection, which flattens the Earth onto a cone. That projection spreads the Antarctica shape across the whole frame, and the land color covers the sea.

Grok inspected its export and did not report the problem. Its documentation does not mention it.

A reader’s run with DeepSeek

On November 11, xiongbiao (@BiaoXiong73311) ran the same brief with a DeepSeek model and posted the poster on X. I did not run this session, and I do not have its files. The post gives these run details: “DSH + Deepseek-v4.1-Flash Max, 22 min 22 s, ¥1.51, 9.25M tok (output 127,936 tok), 80 steps”.

Claude Fable 5.2 compared the numbers on the poster with a new download of the same USGS query on November 11. All of them agree:

  • 515 earthquakes, with 449 shallower than 70 km.
  • 14 deeper than 300 km, and the deepest at 561 km.
  • The counts for the six depth classes.
  • 165 earthquakes in the 36–40°N band, and the two median longitudes.

That check covers the poster only. The code, the saved data, the documentation, and the 540-pixel preview were not available. This run is not in the tables above.

What the later runs showed

Thirteen runs of one brief used the same 515 earthquakes, and every number I could recompute held. The arithmetic was never the weak point. Most errors were in the sentences around the numbers, along with one projection defect and text too small to read on a phone.

The later runs also showed that cost does not follow tokens. Two runs with almost the same tokens differed in cost by four times, and the run with the most tokens was among the cheapest. A strong brief reduced the work needed after delivery. It did not remove the need for review.

Sources and scope

This is a selected earthquake catalog, not a hazard forecast or a map of every earthquake. The magnitude threshold, geographic bounds, year, and profile band limit the story.

  • USGS earthquake catalog and query service. All seven runs saved the source records used in their maps.
  • Rhea et al. (2010), Seismicity of the Earth 1900–2007, Japan and vicinity. USGS Open-File Report 2010–1083–D. Cited by Fable, Grok, Astra, and Sol.
  • Hayes et al. (2018), Slab2, a comprehensive subduction zone geometry model. Cited by Argon and Hydrogen.
  • Bird (2003), An updated digital model of plate boundaries. The source of the PB2002 boundaries that Neon, Argon, and Hydrogen draw.
  • Natural Earth provides the basemap data for all seven posters.
