Create a finished, professional cartographic poster for Day 10,
“Prompting,” of the 2026 #30DayMapChallenge.

This is a one-shot assignment. Make the map autonomously from this brief,
without clarification questions or human feedback. You may inspect and
revise your own work within the time limit.

TIME LIMIT

You have a maximum of 30 minutes of total elapsed time from submission
of this prompt. Planning, research, downloads, tool execution, rendering,
inspection, and delivery all count. Record the start time immediately
and monitor the deadline.

Aim to produce a usable export by minute 20. Reserve the final five
minutes for inspection, corrections, and delivery. At 30 minutes, stop
all work and deliver the best existing result, stating any omissions.
Do not continue background work beyond the deadline.

Use any available tools. Keep the model selected for this session;
do not switch to or consult another model.

THE MAP

Title: “Beneath Japan”

Create a map answering:
Where did earthquakes occur around Japan in 2025, and what does their
depth reveal that a surface map alone would hide?

Audience: an interested general reader without specialist geological
knowledge. The result should be suitable for a professional cartography
portfolio and understandable as a social-media image.

Create one original composition at 2160 × 2700 pixels, portrait.
The geographic map must be the dominant element.

FIXED DATA AND SCOPE

Use the USGS earthquake catalog:
https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/

Retrieve GeoJSON or CSV from its /query endpoint with:
- starttime: 2025-01-01T00:00:00
- endtime: 2026-01-01T00:00:00
- minmagnitude: 4.5
- minlatitude: 29
- maxlatitude: 46
- minlongitude: 128
- maxlongitude: 147
- eventtype: earthquake
- orderby: time-asc

Retain events from the start of 2025 through, but excluding, the start
of 2026, in UTC. Use unique event IDs and verify that the response is
complete. Preserve the downloaded data and retrieval time.

These bounds define the analytical area. You may add geographic context
outside it, but make the coverage boundary clear and calculate all
summary statistics from the defined selection.

Use the catalog’s reported magnitude and hypocentral depth. Label
magnitude “M”; the catalog can contain different magnitude types.
Document any excluded or missing records.

Use real geographic data for coastlines and contextual features.
Natural Earth is a suitable starting point:
https://www.naturalearthdata.com/

Geographic positions, data values, and scientific claims must be
traceable to sources. Any generated decorative imagery must remain
separate from the geographic and analytical content.

REQUIRED CONTENT

1. Main map
Show earthquake epicenters with graduated circles indicating magnitude
and a perceptually ordered color scheme indicating depth in kilometers.
Make overlapping events readable. Use identical encodings in the
legend and supporting graphic. Explain the symbol sizes without
implying that they represent shaking intensity or energy.

Include enough geographic labels to orient an unfamiliar reader:
Japan’s principal islands, surrounding waters, and a restrained
selection of cities or other reference places.

2. Supporting depth graphic
Show longitude against earthquake depth for the subset between
36°N and 40°N, across the same longitude range as the main map.

Put west on the left, east on the right, and increasing depth downward.
Mark this latitude band on the main map. Label both axes and explain
that events across the band are combined into this view. Show the
observed points; do not invent a continuous slab surface.

3. Story
Write a concise subtitle and two short annotations that help readers
interpret specific patterns visible in the data. Include at least one
computed numerical finding. Distinguish observations from geological
interpretation, and support any explanation of subduction with an
authoritative source.

Let the actual data determine the claims.

CARTOGRAPHY AND DESIGN

Include:
- A clear title, geographic scope, date range, and magnitude threshold.
- Complete magnitude and depth legends with units.
- A suitable regional projection, identified in the credits.
- An accurate kilometer scale bar and north indicator.
- Restrained coordinate references and a clear map boundary.
- A small locator inset showing the region’s global context.
- Readable source credits, retrieval date, and software credits.
- “#30DayMapChallenge · Day 10 · Prompting”
- “AI-generated from a single prompt”

Use sans-serif typography only. Choose the palette, composition,
background, visual hierarchy, and annotation treatment yourself.
Build a coherent visual theme around the subject. Make deliberate
use of whitespace and keep supporting information subordinate to
the map. Additional layers must improve the story enough to justify
their acquisition and visual complexity.

Create a new design for this assignment. Keep the artwork model-neutral
so the two results can be compared without their model names.

CHECK BEFORE DELIVERY

Inspect the actual exported image at full resolution and at 540 pixels
wide. Check label collisions, clipping, legibility, symbol visibility,
legend consistency, geographic alignment, scale, and orientation.

Verify that counts, extrema, annotations, and the supporting graphic
agree with the saved data. Correct consequential problems within the
remaining time. State any unresolved limitations honestly.

DELIVERABLES

Save everything in a new output folder:
- map.png — 2160 × 2700 pixels.
- preview.png — 540 × 675 pixels.
- map.pdf or map.svg — a matching print/vector export, preserving
  vector text and graphics where practical.
- The script or editable project used to create the map.
- The downloaded analytical data.
- README.md with source URLs, retrieval time, projection, processing
  choices, reproduction instructions, actual elapsed time, and any
  limitations.

Prioritize a complete, accurate, visually resolved map over additional
features. Finish by displaying the map and linking to the deliverables.
Keep your final explanation under 150 words.
